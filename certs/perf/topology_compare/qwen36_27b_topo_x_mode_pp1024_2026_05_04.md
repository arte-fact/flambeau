# Comparative bench v2: pp1024 prompt, proper pp/tg separation

**Date**: 2026-05-04
**Model**: Qwen3.6-27B-Q4_1
**Hardware**: 4× MI50 (gfx906), 16 GB each
**Knobs**: `FLAMBEAU_CTX_CAP=8192`. "batched" = `FLAMBEAU_BATCHED_DECODE=1
FLAMBEAU_INFLIGHT_SLOTS=4`. Prompt: ~738 tokens (pp1024-class).

## Methodology change from v1

The v1 bench used a 70-token prompt + 2-call estimation that conflated
prefill and decode. Resulting "pp" numbers (~35 tok/s) were dominated
by decode noise, ~10× below reality.

v2 uses a long prompt (~738 tokens) and isolates prefill via
`max_tokens=1`:
- `pp_tok_s = prompt_tokens / time(max_tokens=1)`        → pure prefill
- `tg_tok_s = 128 / (time(max_tokens=128) - time(max_tokens=1))` → pure decode

## Five findings

### 1. Real prefill is ~10× faster than v1's measurement

The v1 bench's "35 tok/s pp" was decode-noise-bound. At pp738:
- pp4:    18.8 →  179.4 (9.5×)
- tp2:    35.0 →  285.0 (8.1×)
- pp2tp2: 35.3 →  292.4 (8.3×)

These numbers are consistent with the AUTO-6 cert's pp512 measurements
(27B-Q4_1 pp2tp2 ≈ 309 tok/s).

### 2. Batched-decode helps at pp4 (1.34×), HURTS at tp2/pp2tp2

| topology | speedup at N=4 |
|----------|---------------:|
| pp4      | **1.34×**       |
| tp2      | **0.92×**       |
| pp2tp2   | **0.95×**       |

Inverse of the part-1 conclusion. With a real prefill workload:
- **pp4 wins**: each PP stage idles 3/4 of the time at N=1. At N=4
  with batched-decode, slot scheduling fills more of those gaps.
- **tp2 / pp2tp2 lose slightly**: prefill is already fast (~290 tok/s)
  and dominates wall time. The batched-decode scheduler's per-slot
  overhead (drain → leader → dispatch coalescing) exceeds the small
  decode amortisation benefit.

### 3. tg numbers unchanged (decode is decode-bound regardless)

- pp4: 20 t/s
- tp2: 26 t/s
- pp2tp2: 28-29 t/s

Pure decode is HBM-bound: it doesn't care about prefill batching.

### 4. VRAM growth is dramatic for batched at long prompts

Peak VRAM grows much more than I expected when prompts are long:
- pp2tp2: idle 22.7 → peak **33.4 GB** (batched 4c, +10.6 GB during 4-conc)
- tp2:    idle 22.3 → peak **27.6 GB** (+5.3 GB)
- pp4:    flat 27.9 (idle = peak; SLOTS=4 KV pre-allocated)

The 4-slot KV cache at pp738 + 64 = 802 tokens stays larger than the
"idle" measurement (which was taken right after model load, before any
chat). That's expected: 4 slots × 802 tokens × layer-dim KV bytes.

### 5. GPU% mean unchanged from v1, confirming bottleneck pattern

- pp4: ~25 % — still under-utilised (each stage idles 3/4 of the time
  in serial-pipeline-without-pipelining). This is exactly the gap
  pipelining (#290/#294) should fill — but the part-5 cert showed it
  delivers 1.0× currently.
- tp2 / pp2tp2: ~44 % — TP collectives spread compute across 2 ranks;
  remaining 56 % is collective-sync + decode HBM stalls.

## So… is batched-decode worth it on the production target?

**For pp2tp2 (the prod target) at 4 concurrent users with long prompts:
NO, it slightly regresses (0.95×).** Part-1's 1.04× came from a tiny
prompt where prefill was lost in noise; once prompts grow, batched-
decode's per-slot scheduler overhead becomes visible.

**For pp4 at 4 concurrent: YES, 1.34×.** PP4 has more idle GPU time
to fill, and batched-decode does fill it.

**The 3× gate is far away** in either case. The lever map:
- batched-decode at N=4 caps at 1.0–1.4× depending on topology.
- #290/#294 pipelining adds at most 1.6× ceiling (not realised yet).
- #288-v2 wave64 routing adds 1.27× on the GDN ssm_out shape only.
- Combined plausible: 1.3 × 1.6 × 1.27 ≈ 2.6× — still under 3×.

The path to 3× requires a structural change: bigger N (>4 — needs
smaller-quant model or smaller ctx), pure-PP=4 with realised
pipelining, or hardware (xGMI / NVLink rig).

## Methodology check: is batched prefill engaged?

YES. `FLAMBEAU_TP_BATCHED` defaults to engaged (`!= "0"`), so prompts
≥ 8 tokens go through `forward_prefill_tp_batched_layers` /
`forward_prefill_hybrid_batched_logits`. The 285-292 tok/s pp on
tp2/pp2tp2 confirms this: a per-token loop at 27B / TP=2 would be
much slower. The pp4 path has no batched prefill (PP-only) — that's
why pp4 prefill (179 t/s) is roughly 0.6× of TP-batched.
